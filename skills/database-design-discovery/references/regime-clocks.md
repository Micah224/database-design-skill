# Regime clocks

Breach-notification, erasure, residency and retention obligations side by side. **A design aid, not legal advice**: confirm each against the current instrument for your jurisdictions. The schema consequence is the same in every row — a breach record needs a per-regime deadline column and a recorded rationale for every notification made or withheld, because you cannot run every regime to the slowest clock.

| Kenya | Data Protection Act 2019 | Without delay, within 72 hours of becoming aware (s.43(1)(a)) | Without delay where there is a real risk of harm | Yes - right to erasure and to object | Section 50 lets the Cabinet Secretary prescribe processing through a server or data centre in Kenya on strategic-interest or revenue grounds. No general localisation mandate has been made under it, though sector-specific transfer restrictions exist (e.g. Civil Registration Regulations 2020). | Retention only as long as necessary for the purpose. | Consent must be express, unequivocal, free, specific and informed. |
| South Africa | POPIA (Act 4 of 2013) | As soon as reasonably possible after discovery, to the Information Regulator | As soon as reasonably possible after discovery, to the data subject | Yes - deletion or destruction where the record is no longer authorised to be retained | Section 72 restricts transfers absent adequate protection, consent or contract necessity. | Retain only as long as necessary unless another law requires longer. | Consent must be voluntary, specific and informed; the responsible party bears the burden of proof. |
| United States - HIPAA | 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164 | HHS within 60 days if 500+ individuals affected; annually otherwise | Individuals without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days | No general erasure right; amendment and accounting-of-disclosures rights instead | No federal localisation requirement. | Documentation retained 6 years (Sec. 164.316(b)(2)(i)); state medical-record laws often require longer. | Authorization is the analogue of consent and is form-specified; treatment/payment/operations need none. |
| United States - state privacy | CCPA/CPRA and successor state statutes | Not in CCPA itself; California Civ. Code Sec. 1798.82 requires notice in the most expedient time possible | Same statute - most expedient time possible, without unreasonable delay | Yes - right to delete, with enumerated exceptions | No localisation requirement. | Disclose retention periods, or the criteria used to set them, at collection. | Opt-out model for sale/sharing, with opt-in for minors; consent is not the default basis. |
| PCI DSS v4.0.1 | Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard | Card-brand and acquirer rules, not a statute - typically immediate | Not applicable; downstream notification follows the applicable privacy law | Not applicable; sensitive authentication data must not be retained after authorisation at all | No localisation requirement in the standard. | Req. 10.5.1: 12 months of audit history, with at least the last 3 months immediately available for analysis. | Req. 10.2.2 fixes the fields every audit record must contain - model them as columns, not free text. |
